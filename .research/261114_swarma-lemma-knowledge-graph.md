# Research synthesis

Draft for review, 2026-11-14.

## Recommendation

Build a small local-first Go module. Record immutable node revisions and intent-bearing events; derive a materialized head/support projection and a separate history view. Use explicit support groups: all premises within one group are required (AND), while multiple complete groups are alternative justifications (OR). A head revision is supported in a dimension if it is an accepted root or at least one accepted support group in that dimension remains valid. Use SQLite plus FTS5 as the first durable application-store candidate, while keeping a durable event log plus in-memory projection as the smaller prototype path.

Keep the first release to one active approved branch per dimension as a provisional default, while allowing multiple explicit support groups inside each branch. Preserve unsupported and superseded revisions; accepted repairs create a new revision under the same logical node. Require a human to accept changes to normative knowledge. Product claims still need source provenance and human judgment; the graph does not prove that a claim is true.

## Prior art

### Prove2Me

The supplied “prove2me” reference identifies as Prove2Me with high confidence: the pronunciation and product spelling match. Prove2Me describes immutable theorem statements, separate proof submissions, reusable theorem results, and proof sketches that decompose a goal into dependent subproblems. Multiple sketches can provide alternative routes; a theorem resolves when a valid route and its imported obligations are complete. Its Lean kernel supplies a verification guarantee that natural-language product claims do not have. Borrow immutable statements, explicit alternatives, and a searchable work frontier; do not borrow its proof-trust claim. Sources: [Prove2Me overview](https://prove2.me/about), [Prove2Me paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28433).

Anthropic’s account of its Fermat formalization describes use of Prove2Me and its graph, but that account is a project report rather than an independent benchmark. No reported scale or speed number is used as a product requirement here. Source: [Formalizing Fermat’s Last Theorem](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formalizing-fermats-last-theorem).

### Truth maintenance and support groups

Doyle’s truth-maintenance work records justifications so belief updates can follow dependency paths. The NASA primer formalizes one justification as a set of required premises and a belief as supported when it has a valid justification; it also warns that “believed” is a system status, not a truth verdict. De Kleer’s assumption-based system preserves multiple support environments. This directly motivates AND semantics within one support group and OR semantics across separate groups. It also motivates keeping contradiction separate from invalidation. Full ATMS environment enumeration is too broad for v1; use one active approved branch per dimension as a provisional default, with explicit support groups. Sources: [Doyle, Truth Maintenance Systems for Problem Solving](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/c76b7ef0-47a3-4afc-b12b-6c8a0c90898a), [NASA, A Beginner’s Guide to Belief Revision and Truth Maintenance Systems](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19930006101/downloads/19930006101.pdf), [de Kleer, An assumption-based TMS](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(86)90080-9).

### Decision records and traceability

A small decision record with context, decision, status, and consequences keeps rationale available when a decision changes. A superseded decision remains useful historical evidence. Bidirectional traceability links a requirement to its parent and allocated children. These patterns support explicit supersession, source spans, and “why does this depend on that?” navigation. Sources: [Michael Nygard on architecture decision records](https://cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions), [NASA Systems Engineering Handbook glossary and requirements-management process](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/system-engineering-handbook-appendix/).

Formal proof resources such as Lean blueprints, Mathlib import graphs, Metamath, and Isabelle's Archive of Formal Proofs make assumptions and dependencies explicit, but their checking applies to formal statements and proofs. Requirements traceability suites and ADRs offer typed links, impact review, and supersession without establishing semantic truth for ordinary prose. Borrow dependency and history patterns, not their verification guarantees or workflow assumptions. Sources: [Lean Blueprint](https://github.com/PatrickMassot/leanblueprint), [Metamath Proof Explorer](https://us.metamath.org/mpeuni/mmset.html), [Isabelle AFP](https://isa-afp.org/help/), [IBM traceability links](https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/engineering-lifecycle-management-suite/doors/9.7.1?topic=requirements-links-traceability).

### Conflict candidates, rationale, and seeding

Treat automated conflict detection as candidate retrieval, not a truth or acceptance mechanism. Start with normalized subject/property/value, units, modality, typed scope, conditions, exceptions, lifecycle, and effective interval; use lexical or semantic similarity only to expand recall. Deterministic checks can reject malformed fields, duplicate IDs, dangling links, and explicitly incompatible single-valued or interval constraints when scope overlaps. Show exact source spans, normalized comparison, scope overlap, and reason. Keep candidate, confirmed, resolved, deferred, and not-a-conflict outcomes distinct, with a human decision before any current knowledge changes. Requirements-conflict research demonstrates structured semantic models and hybrid logic/LLM approaches on study datasets, but those results do not establish precision or recall for this product. Sources: [FSARC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.02255), [ALICE](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10515-024-00452-x).

Use rationale models as a separate layer from normative knowledge. IBIS provides a useful pattern for retaining an issue, options, and arguments; `supports` and `objects_to` links can explain why an option was selected without determining whether a requirement is current. SHACL can validate graph shape and constraints when the graph is represented as RDF; it cannot decide whether a natural-language interpretation is true. Sources: [Kunz & Rittel, Issues as Elements of Information Systems](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/5cj786v8), [W3C SHACL](https://www.w3.org/TR/shacl/).

Seed existing documents through deterministic parsing and staged extraction. Preserve source URI, digest when available, and exact section or line spans; extract atomic candidate claims with explicit kind, scope, condition, modality, and date precision. Keep wishes, alternatives, exceptions, and status updates distinct from accepted requirements. Extraction creates proposals or held records only; a human confirms product scope, authority, currentness, and relation direction. Retain the original source in its existing custody rather than copying it into the graph repository.

### Event history and storage

An append-only event stream plus a query-optimized current projection fits the need to explain how the current graph was derived. Event sourcing has meaningful costs around concurrency, schema changes, and query design, so use it only for the event history and projections required by audit and invalidation. Source: [Microsoft Event Sourcing pattern](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/patterns/event-sourcing).

SQLite is a reasonable first durable store because it runs in-process, supports recursive common table expressions for graph traversal, and provides FTS5 for full-text indexing. Its built-in Unicode tokenizer supports Unicode tokenization; Porter stemming is designed for English, so Russian and mixed-language quality must be measured. WAL allows readers alongside a writer on one host, but SQLite still permits only one writer at a time and WAL does not work on network filesystems. The Go driver is a separate choice: [mattn/go-sqlite3](https://github.com/mattn/go-sqlite3) requires CGO and a C compiler, and FTS5 is enabled through its build tag; [modernc.org/sqlite](https://pkg.go.dev/modernc.org/sqlite) is a CGo-free port. Confirm FTS5 is present in the chosen driver build and supported targets before committing to this store. Sources: [SQLite serverless model](https://www.sqlite.org/serverless.html), [recursive CTEs and graph queries](https://www.sqlite.org/lang_with.html), [SQLite FTS5](https://www.sqlite.org/fts5.html), [SQLite WAL concurrency](https://www.sqlite.org/wal.html), [mattn driver README](https://github.com/mattn/go-sqlite3), [modernc.org/sqlite docs](https://pkg.go.dev/modernc.org/sqlite).

Pure-Go embedded key-value stores such as bbolt or Pebble are alternatives if build-target constraints or measured workload needs outweigh SQLite's relational queries and FTS5. They move more graph traversal, history, and text-indexing work into the application, so do not select them before comparing representative queries and recovery needs. Sources: [bbolt package](https://pkg.go.dev/go.etcd.io/bbolt), [Pebble project](https://github.com/cockroachdb/pebble).

Keep event/support semantics independent of the database. Version persisted event schemas separately from the API; group multi-event mutations under a transaction ID and update the event log plus head, acceptance, and support projections atomically. A durable append-only event log plus an in-memory projection and compact checkpoints is a smaller prototype path. For the persistent application store, use SQLite transactions for event append plus projection update and small in-memory adjacency/support-count indexes for hot impact queries. Keep current-view and full-history FTS indexes separate. Specify incomplete-tail recovery and add replay checkpoints when measured replay cost warrants them. This is a staged architecture recommendation, not a speed claim. Compare the log prototype and SQLite path only against representative query, recovery, and concurrency needs.

### Graph representation and visualization

Use a typed directed multigraph rather than a tree as the stored model. A tree remains a useful view of one selected dependency path, while multiple typed edges let a node have distinct support, contradiction, supersession, and task-reference relationships. Keep topic, module, lifecycle, owner, and risk as facets; use relation types for meaningful links, and do not duplicate a node just because it belongs to several categories. If module architecture dependencies are needed, keep them separate from claim-support edges and never propagate claim invalidation through them. Multilayer-network research is relevant when the same entities participate in different kinds of ties. Store relation types first and expose named filtered views; a specialized multilayer or hypergraph engine is not justified yet. Sources: [Kivelä et al., Multilayer Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.7233), [Property Graph Exchange Format](https://pg-format.github.io/specification/).

The property-graph format defines structural conventions, not this product’s semantics. Every relation needs a documented direction, allowed endpoint kinds, and effect on validity. Ordinary links, contradictions, supersession, and task references must not propagate invalidation.

Keep the stored graph independent from the renderer. Build a versioned export seam for a selected dimension, head revisions, support states, and revision-pinned links. Begin visualization only after the first working prototype. Later candidate views include a dependency path, a focused neighborhood filtered by relation type, and an impact/history view distinguishing supported heads, unsupported heads, and historical revisions. Graphviz dot is a candidate for static layered exports; Cytoscape.js is a candidate for an interactive browser explorer. Neither renderer is selected for the prototype. Sources: [Graphviz dot layout](https://graphviz.org/docs/layouts/dot/), [Cytoscape.js documentation](https://js.cytoscape.org/).

### Dimension-scoped graphs and implementation overlays

The vision now describes one shared node-identity space whose nodes may appear in one, several, or all orthogonal dimensions. Each dimension can hold a disconnected subgraph with its own node projections, links, state, and history. Treat dimensions as runtime data rather than schema enums. Scope CRUD, containment moves, re-linking, support groups, and head/support projections to an explicit dimension; append events so a move preserves its prior location. A change in one dimension does not silently mutate another.

Keep dimension-local workflow state separate from normative validity. A possible later implementation overlay could represent a shared specification node as a story in the implementation dimension and create task nodes there. The specification dimension would retain its original structure. This option adds costs for representation identity, version and event history, cross-dimension invalidation rules, queries, indexing, and concurrent updates. Keep it out of the MVP and do not build a board replacement now.

Use ordinary dimension-projection and dimension-scoped-edge indexes as the baseline. Additional multi-dimensional indexes may speed filtered queries but increase storage and write maintenance; keep them optional until usage and measurements justify them. The mapping of approved branches, conflict status, and invalidation across dimensions remains an owner decision.

### Revision history, support, and deletion

Give each logical node immutable revisions with explicit `based_on` lineage, and let each orthogonal dimension select its own head revision. Version links with their exact source and target revisions; opening an earlier revision defaults to its accepted event sequence and shows its original link set, never later links folded onto that revision. An explicit graph `as_of_seq` shows later edges as separate events with exact endpoints. Creating a revision does not copy or retarget links implicitly; any copy-forward is an explicit event. A node may therefore have different revision heads and relation structure in separate dimensions while retaining one logical identity.

Keep “head revision” separate from derived support validity. A latest revision can be unsupported if it has no active root or complete support group. A latest-only validity model loses this state and also cannot represent dependants that lost support before receiving replacement content. The provisional design reports head selection and support state separately. Invalidation records an event and successor revision, retains the prior revision and its links, and marks the new head unsupported when appropriate. Whether every affected dependent needs a same-content successor revision or only an event plus derived unsupported projection remains open; successor fanout can produce substantial revision churn.

Treat deletion as an auditable edit, never as invalidation: deleting the latest revision in one dimension restores the previous head there; deleting a logical node tombstones it across dimensions; deleting a node in one dimension withdraws only that projection. Record the scope, reason, prior/new heads, and affected links/revisions in the append-only history. Preserve tombstoned content for history; secure physical purge needs a separate retention decision. Reverting a deleted head is not the same as reactivating an unsupported historical revision.

## Design recommendations

1. Give each logical node one stable identity shared across dimensions, and each immutable revision an exact ID and `based_on` link. A changed meaning creates a new revision; dimension projections do not duplicate the node.
2. Record source URI, content digest when available, and an exact locator such as a heading or line span. A digest identifies source bytes; it does not prove a source or claim is true.
3. Represent a support group as one explicit basis over exact premise revisions. Multiple groups supporting the same revision are alternatives. A head revision is supported only when it is an accepted root or a complete valid support group remains in the selected dimension.
4. Define only one propagating relation in v1: depends_on, directed from a support group to a required premise. Traverse reverse adjacency to find affected groups. Reject unsupported cycles and require a chain to reach an accepted root.
5. Keep conflict candidates and human decisions explicit. Candidate detection never confirms a conflict or changes support state; review may record a resolution, scoped exception, deferral, or not-a-conflict result.
6. Keep one active approved branch per dimension as a provisional default. Whether branches are shared across dimensions, and how conflicts and invalidation cross dimensions, remain owner decisions; concurrent scenarios inside a dimension and full ATMS labeling are deferred.
7. Record append-only events for proposal, acceptance, rejection, conflict-candidate review, support-group withdrawal, invalidation, revision-head changes, link changes, deletion, supersession, and regrowth. Keep invalidation distinct from revision rollback, whole-node tombstones, and dimension-only removal; preserve revisions and link snapshots in history.
8. Define regrowth as one accepted replacement revision/root directly anchored to an unsupported historical revision, not as each new descendant or failed attempt. Preserve all earlier revisions and events.
9. Search supported heads in the selected dimension by default. Make unsupported heads, proposals, and historical revisions explicit filters. Add exact-ID, kind, support state, source, scope, and topic filters alongside full-text search.
10. Let task elements carry one or more exact revision references paired with a dimension. A resolver reports logical IDs, revision IDs, head and support state, graph sequence, detail links, and affected task references; unknown ID, denied access, and service outage remain distinct. It must not silently rewrite task content or status.
11. Keep visualization layouts semantically honest: relation type and direction carry meaning; spatial proximity does not.
12. Treat per-dimension representation overlays, such as an implementation story view with implementation-only task nodes, as an optional extension. Keep overlay state distinct from normative support and defer implementation until identity, history, and cross-dimension invalidation costs are resolved.

## Limits and open choices

The existing Markdown corpus was not inventoried for this synthesis, so import cost and source quality remain unmeasured. No latency, storage, corpus-scale, conflict precision, or conflict recall benchmark is claimed; conflict-quality evaluation requires an owner-labeled set. The Prove2Me identification is a high-confidence match, not a measured probability. The support-group recommendation differs from a conservative initial proposal and may differ from the vision's tree-wide wording; resolve that before implementation. Decide whether branches and validity/invalidation are dimension-local or shared, how revision heads are scoped across dimensions, whether unsupported dependants need successor revisions, how deletion handles refs and physical purge, how moves handle nodes with multiple structural parents, whether per-dimension overlays are needed, and whether additional indexes justify their maintenance cost. Search configuration, regrowth-count definition, import policy, acceptance authority, requirement base/derived typing, and replay recovery also need owner decisions. These recommendations remain provisional while these product decisions are open.
